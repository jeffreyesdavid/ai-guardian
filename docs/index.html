How it works

01 · DISCOVER

What AI is here?

AI apps, command-line agents, running processes, AI browser extensions, local model servers, and MCP servers: the plugins that connect agents to your files and accounts.

02 · MAP

What can it touch?

Flags plain-text API keys, agents with access to your whole drive or SSH keys, permission prompts turned off, and AI servers open to your Wi-Fi.

03 · WATCH (v2)

Is it doing something sketchy?

Phone alerts when an AI process reads your keys or wallets, launches a shell, or talks to a server it never has before.

New in v1.1: tested on the index that listed it

The day M8ven added ai-guardian to its MCP Trust Index, I installed M8ven's own ToolCheck server and scanned before and after. ai-guardian caught it at once, and the diff revealed a flaw in my own tool: the agent count dropped after I added one, because live processes shared a total with installed agents. v1.1 fixes that, flags plugins that send data to outside servers, and gives real version-pinning advice.

Why I built this

At Fal.Con 2026, CrowdStrike launched Falcon Guardian to help enterprises discover their AI agents, map what they can access, and stop threats where they run. The idea is right. But the people installing AI agents fastest (freelancers, small shops, students) will never buy enterprise security. ai-guardian brings the same discover → map → watch model to everyone else, for free.
